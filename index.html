          Settings

          Pick where to play. Loading a map clears the bodies and the blood.

          Saves are kept in this browser on this device. Loading one brings back the map, every ragdoll and zombie with their wounds and missing limbs, the vehicles and where you stood. "Last session" saves itself when you leave the page.

          Play in the same world with friends. One of you hosts a room, the others join it with its code. The host's computer runs the world (the bodies, the damage), so the fastest computer should host.

            Alpha 1.1 current

            • Boss fights in VS Undead. Every third wave (3, 6, 9 and on) is a boss instead of a horde: the Big Brute, a hulking zombie about three metres tall. It gets a cutscene: the ground shakes, a huge hand bursts out of the dirt, slams down, the other follows, and it drags itself out of the ground and roars (Space skips it). Its health bar sits at the top of the screen. It stomps after the nearest player and swipes hard enough to throw you back, and every so often it roars and raises 2 to 4 zombies out of the ground around it. Below 40% health it gets angry: faster, harder hits, more roaring. Headshots hurt it most. When it falls, its minions fall with it, and everyone gets a big points bonus.
            • Inventory (I). Your character turning on a stand, wearing the armour you have (a plate carrier, and a helmet once you have 40 or more) and holding what you hold; drag it to turn it. Next to it: your health and armour, every round of ammo you carry and what's loaded, your points, wave and kills in VS Undead, and all your items in slots with your hotbar under them.
            • Minimum graphics preset (Settings, Graphics). Pure performance and nothing else: the lowest resolution, no shadows or reflections, the fewest blood drops, simple ragdoll shapes up close and no grass or ground mist on the Dead Field. It's never picked for you; choose it when every other preset still stutters.
            • Headshots matter in VS Undead. Zombies shrug off much more damage to the body, so a headshot is the way to put them down; enough body shots still drop one, it just takes a lot. A zombie is worth at most 50 points (a few for hits, the rest for the kill, most for a headshot), and the merchant's prices are lower to match.
            • Waves build up slower. The first wave is 2 zombies and each wave after it brings 2 more (with friends there are more).
            • A little more light on the Dead Field. The field is slightly brighter and the fog a touch thinner, and your flashlight is brighter, reaches further and lights a wider area.

            Alpha 1.0

            • VS Undead (VS Undead on the title screen). A co-op zombie mode on its own map: the Dead Field, an abandoned cemetery field at night, no headstones, just dead grass, bare trees, a broken iron fence and thick fog. The zombies come out of the fog from every side in waves, more each time; from wave 3 some of them run, and from wave 4 some wear helmets and vests. You start with your fists and the M1911 with a few spare magazines, and ammo is limited. Play it alone or host a room first and your friends play it with you. If you go down with friends still standing, you get back up when the wave is cleared; when everyone is down, it's over.
            • Points and the traveling merchant. Hitting zombies earns points, a kill more and a headshot most, plus a bonus for every wave you clear. After each wave the merchant walks in from the fog pushing his cart. Press E on him to spend your points on ammo, guns, bandages, a first aid kit, body armour, grenades, mines and a sentry gun. Press G when you're ready; the next wave starts when everyone is (or when he leaves).
            • Supply drops. Every minute or so during a wave a crate comes down on a parachute near one of you, with a red flare smoking on top. Press E to open it: ammo for the guns you carry, and sometimes grenades, a mine, armour, bandages, points or a whole new gun.
            • New guns for VS Undead. An assault rifle with a red dot (full auto, 30 rounds), a submachine gun (very fast, on the pistol's .45 rounds) and a semi-automatic combat shotgun with an 8-shell box magazine. Each has its own reload and aim, and they're only sold by the merchant or found in crates.
            • Proximity mines and the sentry gun. Put a mine down in front of you and it goes off when a zombie steps near (or when you shoot it). Set up the sentry gun and it turns to shoot every zombie it can see, aiming for the head, until its 180 rounds run out. Both are VS Undead only.
            • Vehicles (spawn menu, Vehicles). A pickup truck and a dune buggy. Press E at the door to get in: W and S drive and reverse, A and D steer, Space is the handbrake, C switches between the chase camera and the driver's seat, click sounds the horn, E gets you out. Hitting someone at speed throws them and breaks bones. In multiplayer everyone sees the cars and who's driving.
            • Chat in multiplayer. Press T or Enter in a room to type a message to everyone (on a phone, tap the room indicator at the top). Players joining and leaving show up there too.
            • Saves (Saves in the menu). Save the scene as it is with one click: the map, every ragdoll and zombie with their wounds, missing limbs and armour, the vehicles and where you stood. Saves stay in this browser, so there's nothing to copy. Load one to pick up where you left off; "Last session" saves itself when you close the page.
            • Higher jump. You jump a good bit higher, enough to get onto a waist-high block.

            Alpha 0.9

            • Skyscraper map. Pick it under Maps in the menu. You start on a city street in front of a 20-floor office tower: walk into the lobby, call the elevator, choose a floor and ride up. Every floor is an open-plan office with desks, a meeting room and a lounge. On floor 20 a ladder goes up through a hatch to the roof, with a helipad and the whole city below. Windows shatter when you shoot them or throw someone through them, and the fall is real.
            • Sniper rifle. A bolt-action rifle with a 4–12× scope. Right click to look through it, scroll (or F) to zoom, and hold Shift to hold your breath and steady the drift. It works the bolt after every shot, and each round goes through bodies, armour and glass.
            • Flintlock musket. A musket from the 1700s. The flint sparks, the pan flashes and a moment later it fires a heavy lead ball in a big cloud of white smoke. It isn't very accurate, it now and then only flashes in the pan, and reloading takes about 6 seconds: prime the pan, pour the powder, ram the ball and cock it.
            • Zombies. Spawn them from the spawn menu. They shamble after the nearest living person on their floor, you included, and claw and bite. They feel no pain and don't bleed out, so only a shot that destroys the brain or takes the head off puts them down. Anyone they bite is infected and gets back up as a zombie after they die.
            • New spawn menu. It now looks like the toolbox: pick the Ragdoll (gray head) or the Zombie (green head) card, set how many, armour, behaviour and where, then Spawn. Double click a card to spawn straight away.
            • Your health. Zombies, long falls and grenades going off near you hurt now. Health comes back slowly when you stop getting hurt, and if it runs out you respawn at the start of the map.
            • Patch 1 Getting off the roof. Look at the hatch on the skyscraper's roof and press E to climb back down the ladder to floor 20. The hatch has a handle and a LADDER DOWN sign, and you get a reminder the first time you come up.
            • Patch 1 Only what you can see is drawn. Inside the skyscraper only the floor you're on is drawn (with its furniture, elevator doors and ragdolls), since the floors above and below can't be seen. On the roof nothing inside the tower is drawn, and the far-off city no longer goes through the shadow pass. Inside an office floor that's about a third of the drawing work it was.
            • Patch 1 Faster physics. Each body part is now only checked against the walls and furniture right around it, instead of against most of the map every step. With 10 ragdolls the physics takes about half the time it did.
            • Patch 1 Resting bodies cost nothing. A dead body that has stopped moving is taken out of the simulation until something touches it: a bullet, a blast, the grab tool, another body or you walking into it. Lots of bodies lying around no longer slow the game down.
            • Patch 1 Dynamic resolution (Settings, on by default). When the frame rate drops, the game draws a little fewer pixels (down to 60%) to keep it smooth, and goes back to full sharpness when there's room.
            • Patch 2 Bodies are drawn together. Every ragdoll and zombie, with their wounds, bruises, burns, stumps and gibs, is now drawn in a handful of batches instead of piece by piece. A busy scene needs about 8 times fewer draw calls, so drawing barely gets slower as bodies pile up.
            • Patch 2 Heavy scenes stay playable. When lots of living ragdolls make the physics fall behind, it steps 90 or 60 times a second instead of 120 until the load drops, and it never tries to catch up with more steps than fit in a frame (that used to make a slow frame slower and slower).
            • Patch 2 V-sync (Settings, on by default). Turn it off to let the game run above your screen's refresh rate, so your aim and the physics update more often. It uses more power.
            • Patch 2 The FPS counter is honest. The debug view (F3) could never show less than 20 FPS, so a struggling scene looked steady. It now counts real frames and also shows how long each frame and the physics take and how many draw calls there are.
            • Patch 2 Smarter dynamic resolution. It now notices when the processor, not the graphics card, is what's holding the frame rate back. Fewer pixels can't help then, which is why the resolution didn't seem to move.
            • Patch 3 No more slow motion with lots of ragdolls. When the frame rate dropped, the physics only got a fixed slice of each frame, so with many ragdolls everyone moved in slow motion, and below 20 FPS the whole game slowed down. Now the physics gets the steps real time needs, and the game keeps real speed down to 10 FPS.
            • Patch 3 Physics twice as fast. A hidden check that grew with the square of the number of ragdolls is gone, body parts are only checked against others close by, and limbs only against the ground when they're near it. Each step also does 10 solver passes instead of 18: at 120 steps a second the joints stay just as tight. With 24 ragdolls a physics step went from 7.7 to 3.9 ms.
            • Patch 3 Far and off-screen bodies cost less to draw. Ragdolls in the distance use simpler shapes (about a fifth of the triangles; you can't tell at that distance, and the switch moves further out when you zoom in), and bodies behind you or off to the side aren't drawn at all.
            • Patch 4 Physics on its own processor core (Settings, on by default). The physics now runs on a separate core while the game draws on another, instead of the two taking turns in every frame. In a test with 40 ragdolls the game went from 19 to 43 FPS, and the physics kept stepping 90 times a second instead of dropping to 60. Everything plays the same; what you see of the physics is one frame later, which you can't notice. If your browser can't run it, the physics stays on the main core like before.
            • Patch 5 Multiplayer (Multiplayer in the menu). One player hosts a room and gets a short code; friends type the code to join and play in the same world. You see each other walk, crouch, jump and aim, each in your own colour with your name over your head, holding the item you have out. The ragdolls, their wounds, blood, missing limbs and X-ray, gibs, grenades going off, broken windows and map changes are the same on every screen. A friend's shots hit, wound and dismember just like the host's, and the hit marker shows for whoever fired. Joining a game in progress rebuilds everything that already happened. For now friends can use the pistol, shotgun, sniper rifle and musket, spawn ragdolls and press the buttons; the other items, the elevator and zombies going after friends come next. Up to 6 players.
            • Patch 6 Multiplayer: every item, zombies for everyone, faster updates. Friends can now use every item: fists, grenades, the grab tool (hold, carry and throw bodies), the machete, the medkit and the defibrillator. Zombies chase and attack whoever is closest, friends included. Kills, wounds and bleeding reach your friends much sooner: the host used to resend every wound several times a second, which could back up a home connection for seconds; it now sends about 7 times less, and a ragdoll dying or falling is sent straight away.
            • Patch 7 Multiplayer across different networks. Joining a friend who isn't on your Wi-Fi often failed: when two home routers, a phone hotspot or a school network won't let the computers talk directly, there was no way around it. The game now falls back to free public relay servers, on the same ports websites use so most firewalls let them through. If joining still fails, the Multiplayer menu says why instead of waiting.

            Alpha 0.8

            • Machete. Slash for long, deep cuts that bleed hard and get deeper on the same spot: enough of them chop through an arm, a leg or the neck. Cuts across the wrist, the back of the ankle or the back of the thigh sever tendons. Right click stabs deep into the organs.
            • Medkit. Hold left click on a body part to bandage its wounds, put a tourniquet above a stump, splint a broken bone or dress a burn. Right click gives morphine, R hangs a blood bag.
            • Defibrillator. Hold to charge, let go on the chest. It can restart a heart that has stopped, as long as the heart and enough blood are left. Shocking a beating heart can stop it.
            • Spawn menu (B). Spawn up to 12 ragdolls where you look, in a row, with a helmet and a vest. Armour stops pistol rounds, pellets and shrapnel until it wears out, but the hit still bruises and cracks ribs.
            • Smarter ragdolls. They put their hands up when you aim at them, duck and cover their heads at gunshots and explosions, run or crawl away from you when they are scared, back away from a blade, and press a hand on their worst wound to slow the bleeding.
            • New menus. This title screen with a slow camera around a badly hurt ragdoll, side panels for Settings, Controls and What's new, and a pause menu with Resume and Main menu.
            • Patch 1 Equip from the toolbox. You start with only your fists. Open the toolbox (Tab), pick an item and press Equip (or double click it) to put it on your bar. The bar holds up to 9 items, numbered 1 to 9, and Remove from bar takes one off again.
            • Patch 1 Classic or V2 behaviour. Ragdolls use the Classic behaviour by default. The smarter behaviour is now V2 (experimental), which you choose in the spawn menu, together with its temperament.
            • Patch 1 The ragdoll on the title screen stays on the title screen: Play starts a fresh world.
            • Patch 1 Very Low graphics for slow computers and older phones: half resolution, no shadows and fewer blood drops for the most FPS.
            • Patch 1 The game tells you when a newer version is out, with a button to reload.
            • Patch 2 The X-ray only shows ragdolls in the game, never the one from the title screen.

            Alpha 0.7

            • Phones and tablets. Touch controls with a move stick, drag to look and buttons for each item, played sideways in full screen. Four layouts (Optimal, Classic, Left-handed and your own, made by dragging the buttons), sensitivity and button size settings, and Add to Home Screen as an app.
            • The game detects phones and tablets by itself (Touch controls: Auto, On or Off in Settings).
            • Fix: aiming down sights with the pistol now slows your movement and look like it should.
            • Patch 1 Grenades thrown at the floor stay where they land instead of sliding away; the throw gets softer the further down you look.
            • Patch 2 Hold the throw button to aim the grenade: a dotted arc, a landing ring and a danger circle. A bigger, more realistic explosion with a white-hot core, a larger fireball and a rising smoke column. The patch number now shows under the version.
            • Patch 3 Touch controls no longer turn on by mistake on computers. The X-ray is a compact panel in the top-right corner, and X switches between 3D and Flat. Loading checks your graphics card and processor and recommends a graphics preset.
            • Patch 4 Mac fixes: the mouse locks reliably, the graphics recommendation is more careful with Retina screens and laptop chips, and the game lowers the graphics by itself if it runs under 40 FPS.
            • Patch 5 The game always loads the newest version after an update. On a Mac it plays in the window with the mouse locked.

            Alpha 0.6

            • Grenade shrapnel. About 50 real fragments that wound organs, cut arteries and break bones, plus extra ones aimed at nearby body parts.
            • Stronger blasts. Up close a blast can blow apart hands, feet, limbs and the head, and tear off arms and legs a bit further out. It also ruptures eardrums and concusses.
            • Burns. First, second and third degree burns on the side facing the fireball, with glowing embers and smoke, pain, shock and fluid loss.
            • Shotgun. Pellets lose power with distance, and at point blank several pellets on one part make one massive wound.

            Alpha 0.5

            • Hands. Blocky hands hold every item and follow every animation: magazine swaps, racking the slide, pumping, loading shells and pulling the pin.
            • Fists. Quick jabs and a hard hook that bruise, stagger, knock down or knock out, and can give a bloody nose.
            • Remington 870 shotgun. 8 pellets per shot, an automatic pump with flying shells, and loading one shell at a time.
            • Grenades and explosions. Throw or lob, a 3 second fuse, and an explosion with fire, smoke, shrapnel, knockback and scorch marks.
            • Blood on walls that runs down, bruises that darken over time, and a Flat X-ray view.
            • Patch 1 Bruises are purple instead of pink and darken faster.
            • Patch 2 The version label in the bottom-right corner.

            Alpha 0.4

            • Switching items plays a draw animation: the item comes up from below, overshoots a little and settles.
            • A bigger base and claws on the grab tool.

            Alpha 0.3

            • New toolbox. A square window with categories and spinning 3D previews of every item.
            • Very High graphics with floor reflections, plus more material detail (grime, wear) on higher presets.
            • The grab tool glows and outlines what you hold. Slow motion goes down to 0.01×.

            Alpha 0.2

            • Grab tool. Pick up ragdolls and body parts from a distance, swing them around, throw them and slam them into things to break bones.
            • Exploding body parts. Keep shooting a destroyed part and it bursts.
            • Real bullets that travel with tracers, so you can watch them fly in slow motion.
            • Time controls. Z slows time down, V speeds it up.
            • Hotbar and toolbox, a loading screen, and Settings with Low, Medium and High graphics and gore options.
